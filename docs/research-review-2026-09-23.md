## Usability walkthrough and human research materials

Expert walkthrough only: all 16 articles answer their intended question in the deck or first section, tables are wrapped for sideways scrolling with keyboard focus, downloadable experiment inputs are linked from both applicable guides, and the worksheet now retains actual outputs instead of scores alone. A small screen may still demand substantial horizontal table scrolling. The private Site cannot be used as a public access check. This is **not real-reader testing**. No readers, interviews, completion rates or observed confusion were fabricated.

Later participant protocol, ready for use: recruit 3–5 adults for each task group without calling the sample representative: (a) Canadian small-business buyer, (b) beginner drafting from notes, (c) policy reader, (d) practitioner checking a document assistant. Ask participants to use a personal or approved device and fictional data only. Consent prompt: “We will observe how you use one published guide and record the task outcome and words you choose to share. You may skip a question or stop at any time. Do not enter private workplace or customer information. Notes will be de-identified; tell us if you agree to note-taking and whether you agree separately to recording. Participation or compensation is independent of performance.” Obtain explicit agreement before any recording and disclose amount and source of compensation.

Tasks: for the spreadsheet article, state the ON/Paid total and one row that should not count; for retrieval, identify which case shows a false sense of confidence and explain the permission boundary; for funding, separate gross cash due, hypothetical residual and no-award amount; for beginner notes, identify the unassigned venue call; for a buying guide, specify a test that would cause rejection of a supplier demonstration. Do not tell participants the intended answer first. Record task, participant group, start/end time only if observed, completion criterion, errors, hesitation, exact confusing passage, link/control failure, and change proposed. Interview prompts: “What did you expect this page to answer?”, “Where did you look next?”, “Which claim would you verify before acting?”, “What information is still missing?”, “Could you reproduce the example?”, “What would you do differently?” Coding labels: task completed, unsupported inference, navigation friction, missing context, trust uncertainty, broken resource, and article-specific request. Use anonymized IDs and preserve dissenting observations.
